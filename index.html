EXHIBIT A
01 / The Record

The Facts

DOCUMENTED

She was approximately five years old when her father, Paul Adams, admitted to his Mormon bishop that he was sexually abusing her. That should have been the moment it ended.

The bishop contacted the abuse "help line" established by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. According to the Associated Press investigation, the call was referred to lawyers for the Church, and Bishop John Herrod was told not to report the abuse to police or child-welfare authorities. Herrod later told federal investigators that he had been told, "You absolutely can do nothing." A second bishop, Robert "Kim" Mauzy, subsequently learned about the abuse. He too consulted Church officials and received the same answer.

So nobody called the police. And while adults protected confidentiality, a child remained in the home with the man who was sexually abusing her. The abuse continued for years.

Then the almost unimaginable happened: another daughter was born into that same home. According to the court record, her father began sexually assaulting her when she was six weeks old. Paul Adams abused the infant, recorded his abuse, and distributed child sexual abuse material online.

The Church did not ultimately expose him. Authorities thousands of miles away did. After investigators in New Zealand discovered one of the videos — footage of Adams raping his then-ten-year-old daughter — an international investigation eventually identified him. Federal authorities arrested Adams in 2017.

Seven years had passed since he confessed his crimes to the first bishop.

EXHIBIT B
02 / The Defense

The Church's Position

LEGAL ≠ MORAL

This distinction matters: Paul Adams was the abuser. The Mormon Church did not create his depravity. Horrifically disturbed individuals can exist in any religion, institution, community, or family. But the Church's choices allowed these crimes to continue for an additional seven years — and against a second child.

What this petition condemns is what happened after supposedly responsible adults learned that a child was being sexually abused.

The Church's position has been that its bishops were legally permitted not to report Adams, because Arizona law contains a clergy reporting exemption for confidential religious communications. The Church defended that position through years of litigation, at enormous expense.

This case does not exist in a historical vacuum. The Church's own published history acknowledges that its founder, Joseph Smith, entered plural marriages or sealings with more than thirty women — including fourteen-year-old Helen Mar Kimball, and some women already married to other men. His successor, Brigham Young, continued plural marriage and married at least one fifteen-year-old girl. These are not allegations invented by critics; they are facts documented in records published by the Church itself.

And on July 30, 2026, the Arizona Supreme Court ruled that the First Amendment generally prevents courts and juries from second-guessing a clergy member's application of religious doctrine in deciding whether keeping such a confession confidential is "reasonable and necessary." The result was a victory for the Church in the Adams children's civil case.

But legal does not mean moral.

If an adult learns that a child is being sexually abused, should any religious rule permit that adult to keep it secret? OUR ANSWER IS: NO.

EXHIBIT C
03 / Common Ground

Protect the Child. Every Time.

NO EXCEPTIONS

We can disagree about almost everything else.

We can disagree about God. We can disagree about religion. We can disagree about scripture, salvation, an afterlife, heaven, atheism, agnosticism — or whether human beings have even begun to understand the ultimate nature of our existence. None of us can prove those answers with absolute certainty.

But there is something we do know.

Children are real. Their suffering is real. And our obligation to protect them is real.

No confession, ecclesiastical privilege, institutional reputation, legal strategy, or religious doctrine should ever be placed above the immediate safety of a child.

EXHIBIT D
04 / What We Are Asking

The Demand

TWO COMMITMENTS

We call upon The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints to make two unequivocal commitments.

  1. D–1

    Report every credible case of child abuse to civil authorities — immediately.

    Whenever any bishop, stake president, clergy member, employee, volunteer, attorney-directed help line, or other Church institution receives credible information that a child is being sexually abused or exploited, the Church will ensure the abuse is immediately reported to the appropriate civil authorities — regardless of whether a jurisdiction allows clergy to remain silent. No confessional privilege. No internal investigation in place of law enforcement. No institutional discretion when a child may still be in danger.

  2. D–2

    Publicly acknowledge and apologize.

    For both its handling of the Adams case and the documented marital practices involving adolescent girls under Joseph Smith and Brigham Young — including Joseph Smith's plural sealings to women who were already married.

The demand is not complicated: protect children before protecting the Church. If the Church believes that what happened in the Adams case should never happen again, it should be willing to say so in the clearest possible terms — and change its worldwide policy accordingly.
