The Case, Expanded

Six themes.
One pattern.

The Adams case is the sharpest example, but it is not an isolated one. These six themes place it in context — each grounded in court records, investigative journalism, and the Church's own published history.

Content note: this page discusses the sexual abuse of children.

THEME I
01 / Pedophilia

Minors, Marriage, and the Failure to Protect

DOCUMENTED

This is not an accusation invented by critics; the record is the Church's own. Its published history acknowledges that founder Joseph Smith was sealed to roughly 30 to 40 women — the youngest, Helen Mar Kimball, several months before her fifteenth birthday — and to women who were already married to living men. His successor, Brigham Young, continued plural marriage and married at least one fifteen-year-old girl. Whatever theological framing is placed around these unions, the ages are facts.

The modern institution inherited not the practice but the reflex: when a child's safety collided with the institution's interests, the institution protected itself. In the Adams case, a father confessed to sexually abusing his daughter, the Church's attorney-staffed help line advised the bishop that he could do nothing, and the abuse continued for seven more years — expanding to an infant sibling — until foreign law enforcement, not the Church, ended it. The Associated Press investigation that exposed the help line found it functioned to route abuse reports toward legal risk management rather than toward the police.

The Adams case was not an aberration. It was the predictable output of a system designed to shield the institution first and the child second.

THEME II
02 / Financial Corruption

A $100 Billion Question of Trust

UNAUDITED

The Church requires a full tithe — ten percent of income — as a condition of temple worthiness, collecting billions annually from members including those of modest means. Yet it has not published financial statements in the United States since 1959. Members who fund the institution are not permitted to see what it does with their money.

What is known arrived through a whistleblower: an investment reserve, Ensign Peak Advisors, exceeding $100 billion. In 2023, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission fined the Church and its investment arm $5 million for using thirteen shell companies to obscure the fund's holdings from public disclosure over two decades — a finding of deliberate concealment, on the record, from a federal regulator.

The labor model completes the picture. The sales force acquiring new members — tithe-payers — consists of eighteen- and nineteen-year-olds serving up to two years unpaid, typically funding their own missions. A hundred-billion-dollar institution staffs its growth engine with the free labor of teenagers and asks its poorest members for ten percent.

THEME III
03 / Chauvinism

A Patriarchy by Design

MALE-ONLY

Every position of presiding authority in the Church — prophet, apostle, seventy, stake president, bishop — is reserved for men. Women cannot hold the priesthood, preside over congregations, or sit in the councils where doctrine and policy are made. This is not a cultural lag the institution is working through; it is the institution's design, defended as divine order.

The structure reaches into private life. Until 2019, temple ceremonies had women covenant to "hearken" to their husbands while men covenanted directly to God. Girls and women are routinely interviewed behind closed doors by untrained adult male clergy — interviews that have historically included explicit questions about sexual conduct, a practice that drew organized protest from within the membership itself.

A system in which men hold every key, judge every worthiness, and speak for God to women is not complementary. It is control, formalized.

THEME IV
04 / Psychological Warfare

The Architecture of Persuasion

BY DESIGN

The Church has refined its conversion method for over 150 years, and it is taught, scripted, and rehearsed. Missionaries are trained in a "commitment pattern" — build the relationship first, invite commitments early, and route every doubt toward prayer and feeling rather than evidence. Investigators are taught that a warm emotional response is the voice of God confirming the Church's claims, and that doubt is a spiritual failing in themselves.

The epistemology is self-sealing by design. An apostle famously taught that "a testimony is to be found in the bearing of it" — say you believe until you do. Members are warned away from critical sources as spiritually dangerous, historical problems are reframed as tests of faith, and leaving carries engineered social cost: family strain, community loss, eternal consequence. Critics apply the vocabulary of high-demand-group research — behavior, information, thought, and emotional control — because the pattern fits.

None of this requires bad individuals. It requires only a system that rewards persuasion and punishes scrutiny — refined, generation after generation, into the friendliest hard sell in America.

THEME V
05 / Racism

The Ban and the Folklore

UNTIL 1978

From 1852 until 1978 — through the Civil War, Reconstruction, and the entire civil rights movement — the Church barred Black members from its priesthood and from the temple ordinances it teaches are required for the highest salvation. Prophets and apostles taught, as doctrine, that Black skin marked the curse of Cain, that Black people had been less valiant in a premortal life, and that the ban was the will of God.

The ban ended in 1978, when heaven's timing happened to coincide with mounting public pressure, university boycotts, and the Church's expansion into Brazil. It took another thirty-five years — until a 2013 essay — for the institution to formally disavow the curse-of-Cain and premortal-valiance teachings as folklore, without ever identifying the prophets who taught them as having been wrong.

An institution claiming continuous divine guidance was, on the question of basic human equality, decades behind the society it claimed to be sent to save.

THEME VI
06 / Arrogance

The Doctrine of Exclusivity

MEMBERS ONLY

The Church teaches, in its own canon, that it is "the only true and living church upon the face of the whole earth." From that premise flows a quiet hierarchy of families: only marriages sealed in its temples endure eternally, which means every other family on earth — every marriage, every parent and child bound by love alone — is, by doctrine, temporary.

The arrogance is not abstract. Parents and siblings who are not members in good standing cannot enter the temple to watch their own child be married; for generations, they waited outside. And the Church extends its claim even past death, performing proxy baptisms for the deceased of every faith — including, repeatedly and despite formal agreements to stop, Holocaust victims.

A doctrine that ranks its own families above all others — in this life and the next — should expect to be judged by how it treated the most vulnerable inside its walls. That record is the reason this petition exists.
